# Literature Survey

A literature survey was conducted to review existing platforms that offer career guidance or student records. This section highlights how current platforms operate and details their limitations.

## Comparative Analysis of Existing Platforms

| Feature / Criteria | LinkedIn | Glassdoor / LeetCode | College TPO Portal (e.g. Superset) | LOOP (Proposed) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focus Area** | Global professional networking | Global salaries, company reviews, coding practice | Administrative placement tracking | Campus-specific knowledge sharing |
| **Authenticity** | User-generated (no verification by college) | Anonymous (unverified by specific institutions) | Verified college records only | Verified college-specific user credentials |
| **Granularity of Story** | Brief text posts; unstructured chronological updates | High-level round names; limited year-by-year context | Only list company name, packages, and eligibility | Detailed prompt-based 4-year journey + preparation strategy |
| **Material Sharing** | External links, generic articles | Limited to coding discussions | Not supported | Integrated resumes and study folders |
| **Access Control** | Open to public | Open to public (paywalled/signup) | Locked to active students | Restricted to campus students and alumni |

## Limitations of Existing Systems
1.  **Lack of Context**: A junior preparing for a campus drive needs to know how a specific company evaluated students at *their specific college*. Global portals cannot provide this.
2.  **Unstructured Content**: Posts on LinkedIn are brief, disappear in feeds, and are often written for personal branding rather than structured guidance.
3.  **No Direct Material Linking**: It is difficult to find a senior's resume linked directly to their interview story on existing professional platforms.
4.  **TPO Portal Limitations**: Existing training and placement officer (TPO) portals are administrative (used for collecting resumes and sending emails), not collaborative knowledge bases for juniors to read preparation strategies.
